For most South African private security companies, the days leading up to payday are the most intense administrative period of the month. Operations controllers, HR administrators, and payroll staff work against strict cut-off dates to turn thousands of individual guard shifts into accurate payslips and statutory submissions.

The difficulty rarely comes from the payroll arithmetic alone. Instead, friction arises because the information required to run payroll is fragmented across three disconnected stages: **shift rostering**, **attendance verification**, and **payroll calculation**.

## Where the manual chain breaks down

When a security firm manages 50 to 250 guards across multiple client sites using separate tools, data has to be re-entered or exported at every handover:

- **Roster periods do not match pay periods:** If site rosters are built on arbitrary weekly sheets while payroll runs on a fixed monthly cycle, administrators must manually slice and combine shift schedules at the cut-off date.
- **Unresolved attendance blockers reach finance too late:** If payroll calculation begins while several sites still have unconfirmed shifts, missing Occurrence Book entries, or unrecorded relievers, finance teams must halt the run and chase site supervisors.
- **Complex shift rules are calculated by hand:** Security guards are typically hourly and pay-graded, while office staff are salaried. Guard pay must account for ordinary hours, overtime, Sunday shifts, public holidays, and NBCPSS leave entitlements. Applying these rules manually across spreadsheets increases the risk of human error.

> When sites, team records, rosters, attendance, leave, and payroll share the same underlying database, the roster you publish becomes the timesheet you confirm—and the timesheet you approve becomes the payroll you calculate.

## A structured five-stage payroll workflow

In Plethora's internal operating model, payroll preparation is designed as an explicit, gated sequence rather than an ad-hoc spreadsheet calculation:

1. **Stage 1 — Attendance confirmation:** Guard attendance must be confirmed and approved per site for the pay period. If unconfirmed attendance entries remain open, the system surfaces them as explicit blockers so they can be resolved before calculation.
2. **Stage 2 — Create payroll run:** Once the pay period is opened, the run pulls directly from approved site timesheets and active employee records—including salaried office staff and hourly guards.
3. **Stage 3 — Calculate:** Configured pay grades, overtime rules, Sunday and public-holiday rates, statutory deductions (PAYE, UIF), and approved reliever shifts are applied consistently across the run.
4. **Stage 4 — Approve:** Finance and management review employee cost summaries, contract labour costs, and exception logs before signing off on the run.
5. **Stage 5 — Mark paid & export:** Once funds clear, the period is closed with a full audit trail and statutory export support.

## Why role-based approvals matter across the chain

Connecting workflows does not mean removing human oversight. An operations controller should be able to assign a reliever and capture attendance without having permission to alter pay grades or approve a payroll run.

By separating module permissions—`View`, `Create`, `Edit`, `Delete`, `Approve`, and `Export`—security companies can let operations, HR, and finance work inside the same platform while preserving strict financial control at every stage.
